About Us

Puerto Rico is facing severe environmental challenges due to climate change and degradation, including rising temperatures, stronger hurricanes, coastal erosion, and loss of biodiversity. Given Puerto Rico's unique geography, the island is particularly susceptible to climate-related disasters, and without effective action, the consequences will worsen over time.

The degradation of ecosystems disrupts agriculture, reduces water quality, and diminishes biodiversity, all of which are essential for the island’s sustainability. Additionally, climate change aggravates socio-economic inequalities, as the most vulnerable communities often bear the brunt of environmental damage.

Educating Puerto Rican children about environmental issues and climate change is more important than ever. By teaching students sustainable practices, we can empower the next generation to protect the environment, build resilience, and secure a brighter, sustainable future for Puerto Rico.

Impacts of Climate Change

Weather Events

Puerto Rico is increasingly facing stronger hurricanes and tropical storms as a result of climate change. Warmer ocean temperatures fuel more intense storms, leading to severe destruction of infrastructure, homes, and power grids. Extreme rainfall from these storms also causes dangerous flooding and landslides, further complicating recovery efforts. The intensification of these weather events places additional strain on emergency preparedness and recovery systems, making it harder for communities to bounce back.

Ecosystems and Biodiversity

Rising sea levels and frequent storms are eroding Puerto Rico's coastlines, threatening homes and natural habitats. Coastal areas are experiencing more severe flooding, displacing communities and contaminating water supplies.

Additionally, Puerto Rico's unique biodiversity, including the iconic coquí frog, is at risk due to rising temperatures and habitat changes. Heat and altered rainfall patterns are disrupting ecosystems, driving species closer to extinction and reducing the island's rich biodiversity.

Agriculture

Puerto Rico’s agriculture is highly sensitive to changing climate conditions. Shifts in rainfall, prolonged droughts, and more extreme weather can reduce crop yields, while warmer temperatures invite new pests and diseases that threaten food security. Small-scale farmers, who are vital to the island's agricultural economy, lack the resources to easily adapt to these changes, putting both food supply and local livelihoods at risk.
